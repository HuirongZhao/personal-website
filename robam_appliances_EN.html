Business Research Notes

Starting from the overall beauty & skincare market and progressively narrowing focus to Shiseido's "China · Travel Retail" merged segment—encompassing mainland China e-commerce, offline counters, and travel retail/duty-free channels. This analysis deconstructs the segment's channel structure, performance trajectory, product strategy, and profitability logic through a top-down research funnel.

Layer 01 · Parent IndustryWidest funnel layer · Setting direction
Trajectory of Global Beauty & Skincare Market

Before focusing on travel retail, observe changes in the parent industry: global beauty continues to grow, but the "quality" of growth has shifted—from greater reliance on price increases and premiumization toward more emphasis on price rationality and efficacy validation. Premium beauty in China is also moving from a traffic-driven boom phase into a structural differentiation phase. This layer provides context for subsequent analysis.

Growth Slowdown

Global Beauty Growth Slows but Does Not Decline

McKinsey projects the global beauty market will maintain approximately 5% annual growth through 2030, yet consumers become more price-sensitive. The growth momentum historically supported by pricing power and premiumization is weakening. McKinsey

Logic Shift

"Premium = High Efficacy" Equation Weakens

In skincare, the premium-equals-performance logic is loosening. Consumers increasingly accept lower-priced brands with visible results and clinical validation. Luxury skincare simultaneously faces substitution pressure from medical aesthetics and wellness services. McKinsey

China Market

Premium Beauty Enters Structural Differentiation

China's premium beauty market has become harder to grow solely through "traffic dividends + international brand premium + e-commerce mega sales." Anti-aging and efficacy skincare remain in demand, while Chinese efficacy brands and K-beauty continue to bring competitive pressure to international brands. In 2023, Chinese beauty market share exceeded 50% for the first time. CBNData

Implications for Travel Retail: The industry's "price rationalization" and "structural differentiation" may transmit to duty-free channels—consumers compare prices across channels and may become more selective about pure "international brand premium." This is an important context for understanding travel retail's headwinds in subsequent layers.
Layer 02 · Sub-segmentNarrowing from market to channel
Travel Retail/Duty-Free: A Unique Channel Business Within the Market

Travel Retail (TR) refers to duty-free sales at airports, in-store duty-free shops and similar locations serving traveling passengers. An important difference from regular shopping malls and e-commerce: who you sell to and price flexibility are not entirely brand-controlled—customers are transient passengers, and price advantages derive from duty-free policy. Thus, its business logic benefits from separate analysis.

Compared to regular retail, travel retail adds two unique variables: "passenger flow affected by travel and policy" and "price differential is both attraction and risk"—these can make its volatility higher than domestic channels.

A variable worth including: domestic e-commerce and duty-free may interact within the same segment. The four variables above describe the internal operation of the duty-free channel, yet this accounting fact matters—in Shiseido's organization and financial reports, mainland e-commerce, offline counters, and travel retail/duty-free all belong to the "China · Travel Retail" single business unit (merged March 2025, see Layer 05). So e-commerce and duty-free are not simply "segment-external competitors" but different sales outlets inside the same managed pool, with both diversion and coordination effects:

① Diversion: Duty-free's appeal is often "cheaper than regular price," yet China's Double 11 and 618 discounts can be substantial; stacked with platform coupons, livestream subsidies, and gifts, Tmall/Douyin delivered prices may approximate duty-free pricing. Consumers may buy similarly priced goods without flying to Hainan, without quota and unit limits, creating some diversion in duty-free passenger flow, conversion rates, and transaction values. Based on annual customs items / shoppers, Hainan's 2024 average items per shopper was about 5.8 units, down from the 10+ unit range seen in 2021–22; e-commerce convenience and pricing may be one influence.

② Synergy: Because both are managed within one business unit, whether consumers order from e-commerce or duty-free, sales are still recorded within the same business unit. This was one reason for merged management—unified pricing and reduced channel friction may help e-commerce and duty-free form a more coordinated role split. Shiseido's 2025 segment recovery was attributed to "e-commerce rebound + offline market warming" jointly.

So a fuller understanding: "Price differential" is better benchmarked against both regular counters and mainland e-commerce big-promo delivered prices; the management goal is to coordinate pricing and channel roles within the segment, keeping consumers within its own pool where possible. This also echoes Layer 01's point that consumers compare prices across channels and may become more selective about pure international brand premium.

Cycle positioning: from dividend to adjustment to stabilization

2020–2022 · Dividend Period

Pandemic Catalyzed Hainan Duty-Free Boom

Outbound travel restrictions, consumption returned to Hainan. Duty-free quota increased to 100,000 RMB annually, Hainan became the engine driving Asia-Pacific travel retail growth. Shiseido entered Hainan market in 2010.

2023–2024 · Deep Adjustment

Triple Pressure Compounded Decline

① Outbound tourism recovery may divert some customers (China national inbound/outbound pax YoY +70.9% in 2024 H1) National Immigration Administration; ② Hainan offshore duty-free per-capita spending fell about 26% 21st Century Business Herald; ③ Japanese nuclear wastewater discharge damaged Japanese brand credibility.

2025 H2–2026 · Initial Stabilization

New Policy + Comprehensive Closure Signals Repair

Offshore duty-free new policy (Nov 2025) expands product categories and eligible people; Hainan comprehensive closure (18 Dec 2025) post-implementation duty-free on imported goods. Post-policy H2 sales YoY +22.6%, Shiseido 2026 Q1 also mentioned positive Hainan recovery signals.

Layer 04 · LandscapeMarket competitors
Competitive Landscape: Two Scenes, Two Playbooks

"China · Travel Retail" segment spans two competitive scenes with different intensities: Mainland e-commerce/offline faces fast domestic-brand growth and pricing promotion pressure—one important pressure source for Shiseido; duty-free channels is where premium international brands compete on channel quality, product mix, and pricing discipline. This section first examines mainland competition, then duty-free.

Scene One · Mainland E-commerce / Offline: Chinese Brands Grow, Japanese Beauty Under Pressure

Mainland is a competitive scene for Shiseido. Chinese beauty brands' market share exceeded 50% in 2023; Pola ranked first in Tmall Double 11 for three years running, Hanskin performed well on Douyin; while Japanese beauty's relative ranking is under pressure—2025 Double 11: Shiseido main brand ~17th on Tmall, ~TOP20 on Douyin; CPB ~11th. Shiseido's premium brands (CPB/NARS) show growth in big sales, yet main brand SHISEIDO has stayed outside Tmall beauty TOP 10 for three straight years.

China · Travel Retail Segment Performance Tracking: 2024 → 2026 Q1

Shiseido's travel retail business declined notably in 2024, gradually recovered through 2025, and returned to positive growth in 2026 Q1, tracing a stage-by-stage recovery pattern. A significant accounting change in 2025 should be noted: "China business" and "travel retail business" merged into single business unit, with combined segment data now including mainland e-commerce, offline, and duty-free three channels; "segment growth rate" ≠ "duty-free growth rate."

This segment contains three channels: ① Mainland online e-commerce (Tmall, Douyin, etc.), ② Mainland offline (department store counters, etc.), ③ Travel Retail/Duty-Free (Hainan, airports, in-store duty-free, Hong Kong).
